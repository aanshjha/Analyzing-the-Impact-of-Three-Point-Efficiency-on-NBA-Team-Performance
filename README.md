# Analyzing the Impact of Three-Point Efficiency on NBA Team Performance

This research examines how three-point shooting volume and efficiency relate to NBA team success across the three-point era. The analysis covers team-seasons from 1980 through 2024 and uses team Win Shares as its primary measure of performance.

## Research questions

1. How have NBA three-point attempt volume and shooting efficiency changed over time?
2. How strongly do three-point volume and accuracy predict team performance?
3. Does the effect of shooting more three-pointers depend on shooting accuracy?

## Methodology

Player-level totals, shooting statistics, and advanced metrics were aggregated to the team-season level. The study uses:

- Descriptive trend analysis of three-point attempts per game and three-point percentage
- Ordinary least squares regression with season fixed effects
- An interaction term between three-point attempts per game and three-point percentage
- Comparative tests and a defensive-rating robustness check where data were available

## Key findings

- NBA teams increased their average three-point volume from roughly 2–3 attempts per game in 1980 to more than 35 by the mid-2020s.
- League-wide three-point accuracy rose from about 28% to approximately 36% over the same period.
- Higher three-point volume and higher accuracy are both positive, statistically significant predictors of team Win Shares.
- The interaction between volume and accuracy is not statistically significant, suggesting that their estimated effects are largely additive.
- Three-point shooting is important, but it does not explain team performance by itself; defense, two-point scoring, turnovers, rebounding, injuries, and other factors also matter.

The results describe associations rather than proving that increasing three-point attempts or accuracy directly causes additional wins.
